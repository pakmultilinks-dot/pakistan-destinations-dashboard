What the data shows

This dashboard covers 24 of Pakistan's most-visited destinations in all six regions: 8 in Gilgit-Baltistan, 8 in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, 3 in Punjab, 2 in Azad Jammu and Kashmir, 2 in Balochistan and 1 in Sindh. For each one it records region, type, altitude, best season, peak months, a typical stay, whatever visitor figure is actually published, and the Booking.com property count. Every figure comes from a public source listed below; where no figure is published, the table says so instead of estimating one.

Visitors: international arrivals grew from about 383,000 in 1993 to 1.9 million in 2018, with a sharp dip to 565,212 in 2013 and a recovery after 2015. Domestic tourism is far larger: about 50 million domestic trips were estimated in 2014, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa alone recorded 9.3 million tourists from 1 May to 31 July 2024. The most detailed official data is for Gilgit-Baltistan: 482,617 domestic and 13,715 foreign tourists in January-June 2026, against 989,793 domestic and 24,139 foreign in all of 2024.

Seasonality: tourism in the north is strongly seasonal. In Gilgit-Baltistan, June alone brought 200,680 of the 482,617 domestic tourists in January-June 2026 (41.6%), while January brought only 18,400 (3.8%). Diamer district (Fairy Meadows and the Babusar route) recorded the most visitors in that period (127,044), followed by Skardu (97,688) and Hunza (86,975). The pattern matches the best seasons in the dataset: May to October for the northern valleys, June to September for the high meadows, and November to February for the lowland cities and Gorakh Hill.

Accommodation: supply is very uneven. On Booking.com (17 Nov 2026) Lahore lists 1,208 properties, Murree 174, Skardu 93, Hunza 38, Naran 28 and Gilgit 26. These counts include nearby properties and measure listings, not rooms or beds. For 18 of the 24 destinations no Booking.com count was collected, and no tourism authority publishes hotel or room counts per destination, so the table records that gap plainly.

What this means for Travel.pk: push northern packages for June to September, when demand peaks; sell the cities (Lahore, Taxila) and Gorakh Hill for the winter months, when the north is quiet; and treat Skardu and Hunza as the most bookable northern hubs, since they combine the highest district visitor counts with the most listed accommodation outside Lahore and Murree.

Key findings

  • International arrivals rose from 383,000 (1993) to 1,900,035 (2018), with a dip to 565,212 in 2013.
  • In GB, June 2026 had 10.9 times January's domestic tourists (200,680 vs 18,400).
  • Diamer district led GB in Jan-Jun 2026 with 127,044 domestic tourists.
  • The UK was the largest source of foreign arrivals in 2024 (503,383), ahead of Afghanistan (461,199) and the USA (260,406).
  • Booking.com lists 1,208 properties in Lahore but only 26 in Gilgit and 38 in Hunza.

Limitations, read before reusing these figures

  • No official visitor count for a single destination is published for any of the 24; the best available are district totals for GB and a provincial total for KP.
  • Booking.com counts cover only 6 destinations, count listings (not rooms or beds) and include nearby properties.
  • Stay lengths are itinerary norms, not measured data; no length-of-stay statistics are published per destination.
  • The GB 2026 figures cover January to June only; the full year is not yet published.
